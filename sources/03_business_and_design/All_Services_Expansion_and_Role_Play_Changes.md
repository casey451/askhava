## Who can join

About 95 kinds of service business fit the model, in 10 groups; most have local businesses in Havasu today, and the lake and part-time owners make boat and property-care services unusually strong here.

**Why Havasu demand is different:**

- **Part-time owners.** About 9,400 of 35,410 homes (26.5%) had no full-time household at the 2020 Census. They need home watch, cleaning, pool and yard care while away. ([Wikipedia, from 2020 Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Havasu_City,_Arizona); my calculation)
- **Vacation rentals.** About 2,000, of which about 1,200 are registered, per city officials. Each needs turnover cleaning, pool care and repairs. ([Mohave County Housing Study, p. 22](https://www.mohave.gov/media/ybcmgt44/mohavecountyhousingreport0927234db9d898-fa49-41c6-bb79-12395dae97fe.pdf))
- **Pools.** The city issued 219 residential pool and spa permits against 99 new-house permits from July 2025 to Feb 2026. ([City permit report](https://www.lhcaz.gov/DocumentCenter/View/5282/February-2026-PDF))
- **Heat.** Average highs of 105–109°F from June to August: AC, solar screens, misting, shade. ([usclimatedata](https://usclimatedata.com/climate/lake-havasu-city/arizona/united-states/usaz0372))
- **Boats.** The tourism bureau calls Havasu "The Personal Watercraft Capital of the World." Boat repair is the largest lake category on your own directory. ([GoLakeHavasu](https://www.golakehavasu.com/pwc))

## License check per category

Every category falls under one of three rules: no state license (city business license only), an ROC class, or another state agency. The app checks whichever applies. Every business inside Lake Havasu City needs a city business license, and the city publishes the active list as a spreadsheet ([city page](https://www.lhcaz.gov/175/Business-License)). That gives the app an identity check even for unlicensed services.

| Rule | Categories | Agency and lookup | What the app checks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| No state license | House, carpet and window cleaning; pressure washing; trash cans; pet waste; lawn, garden, shrub and tree **maintenance**; weekly pool cleaning (attorney to confirm); car and boat detailing; mobile mechanic; boat repair; boat and RV storage; in-state movers; junk removal; pet grooming, sitting and training; home watch; rental turnover cleaning; photographers, DJs, event rentals; locksmiths | City business license ([active list, XLSX](https://www.lhcaz.gov/DocumentCenter/View/3254/Active-Business-Licenses-XLSX)); gardeners are exempt from the ROC under A.R.S. 32-1121(A)(16) | Business name on the city list, plus the phone or email identity check |
| ROC class | Plumbing R-37; HVAC R-39; electrical and solar R-11; pool repair R-6; hardscape and irrigation R-21; roofing R-42; painting R-34; flooring R-8, tile R-48; window film R-65; water softeners R-54; appliances R-63; low-voltage and cameras R-67; garage doors (class unclear) | [ROC search](https://azroc.my.site.com/AZRoc/s/contractor-search) or the ROC's [downloadable lists](https://roc.az.gov/posting-list) | Class and status, as v9.3 does. Under $1,000 with no permit, unlicensed handymen may quote |
| Another state agency | Pest control and weed spraying (Dept of Agriculture); alarm systems and home inspectors (Board of Technical Registration); property management (Dept of Real Estate); septic pumping (ADEQ); wells (ADWR); towing (DPS); mobile vets, massage, mobile hair and nails, catering | Each agency's own search, for example [Agriculture](https://searchagriculture.az.gov/mastercontent/licsearch.aspx), [BTR](https://btr.az.gov/public/registered-professional-search), [ADRE](https://services.azre.gov/publicdatabase/) | One more lookup site per agency for Cowork |

**Three rules v9.3 is missing:**

1. **Gas work is never handyman work, at any price.** The same goes for hardwired smoke alarms and fire sprinklers ([A.R.S. 32-1121(D)](https://www.azleg.gov/legtext/57leg/1R/bills/hb2120p.htm)). A gas water heater or gas pool heater must go only to a licensed business, and a handyman quote on it is refused.
2. **The handyman limit is still under $1,000,** counting labor and materials, with no permit and no splitting a job. Bills to raise it to $2,000, $2,500 and $3,500 failed in 2023, 2024 and 2025.
3. **Weed spraying is a pest-control job unless it's incidental.** A gardener may spray on the same visit, but not pre-emergent, not as an advertised service, and within small amounts ([A.R.S. 3-3613](https://law.justia.com/codes/arizona/title-3/section-3-3613/)). A request for "pre-emergent" or "weed control" goes only to licensed pest-control businesses.

**Suggested launch set:** start with the no-license and ROC categories plus pest control. Leave out until later: childcare, home care, massage, mobile hair and nails, mobile vets, catering, towing and property management. Each needs a separate board check, and several involve children, vulnerable adults or hands-on care, which raises the stakes of a bad match.

Outside city limits (Fort Mohave is unincorporated; Bullhead City and Kingman keep their own lists), the city-license check needs each town's own source.

## Fees

**Recommended price structure: $25 / $75 / $199, by the size of the chosen quote.** Every tier costs less than what the same customer costs a business to win through Google, by published figures. Within that promise, it's the most profitable set tested.

| Tier | Chosen quote | Fee | What it beats (sourced) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small | Under $500 | $25 | One Google Local Services lead costs $30–$162 in every trade with data except pest control ($20–$30), where $25 is about one lead and well under the $46–$68 it costs to win a client. With us, $25 buys a customer who already chose you |
| Medium | $500–$1,500 | $75 | Winning one customer through Google Local Services costs at least $77 (handyman) and up to $369 (roofing) |
| Large | Over $1,500 | $199 | Surveyed cost to win one paying customer: $218 electrical, $263 plumbing, $288 HVAC; roofing about $369 |

**The rules that make it a better deal, in one line each:**

- Pay only when a homeowner picks you and you confirm. Never for a lead you lose.
- No percentages: the fee depends only on which band your quote falls in.
- Ongoing services count as 2 months of the price. A $150-a-month pool client pays $25.
- Automatic refund if the homeowner can't be reached or cancels before you talk.
- Repeat work from the same customer is never charged.

**What the promise costs you.** At 10 requests a day, the middle assumption gives:

| Option | Profit per year | Picks accepted | Better than Google for every trade with data? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **$25 / $75 / $199** | **$31,400** | **80%** | **Yes** |
| $25 / $75 / $199 with a 10% cap | $30,600 | 86% | Yes, but invites low quotes |
| $25 / $75 / $300 | $34,300 | 79% | No ($300 is above the $218–$288 survey figures) |
| $19 / $59 / $199 | $28,000 | 87% | Yes |

It earns about $3,400 a year more than $19 / $59 / $199. Most of the declines are jobs under about $250 (window cleaning, small appliance and handyman jobs), where $25 is a big share. Businesses that don't want those can untick them on their checklist.

**How the cost-to-win figures were worked out.** Google Local Services lead prices by trade come from 100+ clients of one agency ([The Media Captain](https://themediacaptain.com/google-local-service-ad-statistics/)). They're divided by the 43.9% booking rate from 888 contractors ([Pipelineon, citing SearchLight Digital, Feb 2026](https://pipelineon.com/blog/home-service-marketing-benchmarks-2026/)). A booked appointment isn't a won job, so the real cost is higher, which makes the comparison conservative. The $218–$288 figures are a March 2026 survey of 760 businesses, from the same page. There's no data yet for painting, remodels, boat work or movers. Once the pilot runs, your own records (average fee per won job, by trade) become the proof.

**Odd quotes:** a price range uses its midpoint. A trip-fee-plus-estimate quote uses the tier set for its category, so a business can't drop to $25 by quoting only a trip charge.

**Quotes just under a line:** the fee changes only at $500 and $1,500, so there's no reason to shade a quote except right at those lines. The owner portal flags a business whose quotes keep landing just under them. The day-3 PROBLEM reply catches a final bill well above the quote.

**How the model works:**

- 25 categories in this mix: 45% repairs, 24% recurring services, 31% other one-time jobs.
- Typical prices come from the research below, with real-world spread.
- A business is more likely to decline as the fee grows as a share of the job. "Half would decline" is set at 10%, 15% or 20% of the job (the three columns).
- The goal was about 90% of picks going through; the tier cut-offs at $500 and $1,500 came out best whenever the model could reach that goal.
- About 1 in 4 requests ends in a pick; 10% refunds; Stripe fees; 55¢ per request in texts and AI; $157 a month fixed.

The decline rates are assumptions, not data. Use the dollar figures to compare options, not as a forecast. During the hand-run test, track how often businesses decline at each price level. If declines stay low, raise the medium and large tiers.

**The local anchor.** A Lake Havasu-area pool route was listed at $79,680 for 57 pools: about $116 a month, or $1,400 a year per pool. It was priced at 12 months of billing, so one month of service is what a pool company already pays to buy a client ([BusinessBroker.net listing](https://www.businessbroker.net/business-for-sale/pool-service-route-in-lake-havasu-for-sale-havasu-city-arizona/589638.aspx), undated, sold). Counting repairs, the route's listed revenue was $101,000 a year, about $1,770 per pool. Your $2,500-a-year guess fits a large pool or a client who buys a lot of repairs, not the typical one.

## What the design must change

Opening the app to any service changes 9 parts of v9.3. The core (anonymous job cards, every quote shown, pick by code, confirm then pay, masked calls) stays the same.

1. **A category catalog replaces the 5-trade list.** Each category stores its job types, its license rule (none, an ROC class, or another state agency), whether it's recurring, its intake questions, its fee, and whether after-hours emergencies apply. Adding a category becomes a settings change, not new code.
2. **Recurring quotes.** Quote cards get a new type, RECURRING PRICE: price per visit or per month, how often, and any contract length. One-time jobs keep FIRM PRICE, PRICE RANGE and TRIP FEE + ESTIMATE.
3. **Fee set at the pick from the category and the quote.** This generalizes v9.3's $149 rule. Recurring services are priced on yearly client value (see Fees).
4. **Early-cancel refund for recurring services.** At day 30 the homeowner gets one text: "Still using Desert Pool Care? Reply YES or NO." A NO gives the business a partial refund automatically. The rule needs your sign-off (see Decisions).
5. **License check only where Arizona requires one.** ROC categories keep the current checks. Categories licensed by another agency (pest control, for example) need a second lookup site added to Cowork's checks. Unlicensed categories skip the license step but keep the identity check.
6. **The $1,000 handyman limit applies only to construction-type work.** It's an ROC exemption. A $1,200 car detail or move is not refused.
7. **Location isn't always a house.** Boats, cars and RVs may be at a marina, storage lot or launch ramp. The job card shows the town and the kind of place, never the exact spot, same as now.
8. **More categories means more chances to misfile a request.** Each category gets its own keyword list, and the "Which fits best?" question covers any two close categories. A request that fits no open category is answered right away and logged as recruiting demand, as v9.3 already does.
9. **Texting registration describes local services in general,** not only home repair. Same use case (Customer Care), same opt-in, new sample messages. Twilio support should hear the broader model before registering.
