# Hand-run test: prove it by hand before the build (D-455, D-468)

**For:** Casey. **When:** now, before you reply `approved to build` at Gate P. **How long:** 6 weeks (8 if the paid weeks start late). **Cost:** about $300 to $450 before week 4: the Lake Havasu City business license ($196 new, $140 a year to renew), the LLC filing ($50, or $85 expedited) and a Maricopa or Pima County statutory agent (about $50 to $150 a year), plus a mailbox if the agent's address can't go in email footers; then Stripe's card fees on real introduction fees (section 9). **Your time:** about 6 to 8 hours a week at first, which should fall as setup and recruiting settle.

The build is held at Gate P until this test answers four questions with real people:
1. **Demand:** will homeowners who don't know you send a job to an unnamed local service?
2. **Choice:** in the trades you push, do most requests get **2 or more** quotes within 24 hours?
3. **Payment:** will a picked business actually **pay** the introduction fee? Saying "it would be worth it" doesn't count.
4. **Time:** does running it fit in your 20 to 40 hours a month once the start-up work is done?

Nothing is texted and nothing is automated. You do every step by hand with free web forms, one email inbox and one spreadsheet. The same rules as the real service apply (section 2). This is a winter test: whatever it shows describes winter Havasu, not the whole year (section 3).

What this test can and can't show: with about 25 to 40 requests, the numbers are signals, not proof. Seventeen quoted out of 24 reads as 71%, but the real rate could be anywhere from about 50% to 85%. So the scorecard asks for clear signals and minimum counts, not exact percentages.

## 2. Rules that hold even by hand

- **Ask Hava is not a contractor.** You never bid, set a price, schedule, supervise or promise anyone's work. Every message says the homeowner hires the business directly.
- **No ranking.** Homeowners get every quote in the order it came in, in the same format, with no "top pick". Never write "best", "vetted", "trusted", "recommended", "top-rated", "leads", "qualified", "ready to hire", "guaranteed", "referral", "exclusive", "concierge" or "connect you with". For licensed work, the true line is: "For work that needs an Arizona contractor license, we send requests only to businesses whose ROC license we've checked." A license check isn't a guarantee of the work.
- **Homeowners never pay.**
- **No marketing texts and no calls to businesses.** Everything goes by email and the web forms. You may visit a few businesses in person (section 8). Don't recruit by phone: Arizona's telephone-seller law (A.R.S. 44-1271, 44-1272) may require registration first, and the Secretary of State won't say in advance whether it applies to you (D-374).
- **Say who you are.** Every post and every reply in a group starts with "I run Ask Hava" (FTC endorsement rules, pack file 10 section D2). One real profile. No second accounts and no anonymous posts.
- **Post by hand.** Facebook bans automated posting to groups. AI may draft a post; you paste and post it yourself.
- **Never pay a group admin** to let you post. Meta bans selling permission to post.
- **Homeowner details stay in the sheet.** Never paste a homeowner's name, email, address or photos into an AI tool (`BUILD_BRIEF.md:66`). Remove them first if you want AI help wording a job card.
- **The homeowner's details go only to the business she picks:** her first name, the job address and her email, once she says yes, and in the paid weeks only after the fee is paid. Never her phone number (`BUILD_BRIEF.md:151`).
- **Licensed trades go only to licensed businesses.** Check the ROC number by hand (section 4, step 5). Pest control: the business tells you its Department of Agriculture license number, and the card says it isn't independently checked (D-417).
- **Delete what you don't need.** 90 days after the test ends, delete homeowners' names, addresses and emails from the sheet and the inbox. Keep the rows with those cells blank so the numbers survive.

## 3. The test's rules, fixed before it starts

Write these into the sheet's first tab before the first post, and don't change them mid-test. If something comes up they don't cover, decide it, write it down with the date, and apply it to every later case.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Request** | One real job, from one household, in Lake Havasu City. A second job from the same household counts separately; the same job sent twice counts once. Tests, jokes and out-of-town jobs don't count. |
| **Source class** | Every request gets exactly one: **Seeded** (someone you asked, or who knows you: friends, family, neighbors, coworkers); **Partner** (a property manager, rental host or realtor you lined up); **Organic** (found Ask Hava on their own: a post, a thread, a friend of a friend). The request form's "How did you hear about Ask Hava?" question is required and decides it. When unsure, it's Seeded. |
| **Routable** | A request in a trade with at least one active business whose license covers the job. |
| **Pushed trade** | One of the one or two trades your posts push (section 7), fixed at the start of week 1. |
| **Valid quote** | A price (fixed, range, or "visit needed, $X to look") and when they could do it, received within 24 hours of the card being sent. |
| **Quote clock** | Starts when you send the card email; ends 24 hours later. |
| **Active business** | One that has sent at least one valid quote. A sign-up alone doesn't make a business active. |
| **No pick** | The homeowner hasn't picked 3 days after the last quote window ends. Ask her once why, then close it. |
| **Nudge** | Anything you do beyond the standard emails: a reminder to a business, a personal message, a visit, a call from them that you answer. Log every one in the Nudges tab: date, request or business, what you did. |
| **Season** | Every weekly row carries its dates. The result is reported as a "winter test", because Havasu's winter visitors raise the population a lot from about November to March. |

## 5. Running a job by hand

| Step | What you do | Template |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Request comes in | Read it within a few hours. Set its source class and whether it's routable. If no business covers it, reply with template A and mark it "not covered". Start a timer: note your minutes on every request. | A |
| 2. Questions | If key facts are missing, ask up to 3 follow-up questions by email, the way a good receptionist would (sizes, age of the unit, access, photos). The question bank in `docs/catalog/question_bank.yaml` lists the right ones per trade. | B |
| 3. Job card | Write the anonymous card. No name, no street number, no email. Send it to the homeowner first: "Here's your request as businesses will see it. Reply if anything is wrong. It goes out in 30 minutes." Note whether she corrected anything. | C |
| 4. Send it | Email the card to every signed-up business whose kinds of work and license cover it, in BCC. Note the time sent. | D |
| 5. Quotes | Businesses reply with a price and when they can come, plus a short note. Note the time each one arrives. An unlicensed handyman may quote only jobs under $1,000 in total (labor and materials) that need no permit and involve no gas, hardwired smoke alarms or fire sprinklers (LIC-019 to LIC-025); a handyman quote of $1,000 or more isn't passed on, and you tell the business why. Never split one project into smaller jobs to fit under the limit. Work that usually needs a permit or a specialty license (electrical panels, gas lines, water heaters, HVAC, roofing, structural work) goes only to licensed businesses, whatever the price. In weeks 4 to 6, don't remind businesses to quote. | |
| 6. Quotes to the homeowner | Email her every quote in arrival order, same format, with each business's license number and its Google and Yelp links. Send the first one as soon as it arrives, then the rest together. When the 24 hours end, send template J once. | E, J |
| 7. Pick | She replies with the business she picks and says yes to sharing her first name, the job address and her email. | |
| 8. Introduction | **Weeks 1 to 3:** email the picked business her details and ask: "If this weren't free, would it have been worth $<fee>?" **Weeks 4 to 6, pushed trades:** only to a business that accepted the paid-pilot terms. Email the payment link first (template K); send her details only once it's paid, then the receipt (template L). If they don't pay within 4 business hours, offer the pick to the homeowner's next choice and note the refusal. Tell the others the job is no longer open, and whether the picked quote was lower, about the same or higher. | F, G, K, L |
| 9. Day 3 | Ask her: "Did <business> get in touch? Reply DONE, NO or PROBLEM." Only after a DONE, ask whether you may share what she says on askhava.com with her first name and town (no reward, ask everyone who replies DONE). | H, I |

**Templates** (plain, short, signed "Ask Hava"):

- **A, not covered:** "Thanks for sending this. We don't have a <trade> business on Ask Hava yet, so we can't get you quotes for this one. We've noted it so we can add businesses. If it can't wait, it's fine to call a company you know."
- **B, questions:** "Thanks. To get you useful quotes, a few quick questions: 1) ... 2) ... 3) ... Photos help too, if you have them. Reply to this email."
- **C, card to the homeowner:** "Here's request <n> as businesses will see it. Your name and contact details aren't on it. Reply if anything's wrong; otherwise it goes out in 30 minutes. <card>"
- **D, card to businesses:** subject "Ask Hava job <n>: <trade>, <job type>, Lake Havasu City". Body: the card, then "Reply to this email with your price, when you could do it and a short note. Sent to <N> businesses. <Free during the test. / If you're picked, the introduction fee is $<fee>, paid before you get the details.> Ask Hava is not a contractor; the homeowner hires you directly." Then this footer, word for word: "You're getting this job card because <business> signed up for Ask Hava job cards. Ask Hava LLC, <postal address>. To stop getting them, reply STOP or UNSUBSCRIBE, or email <help email>." Honor a stop at once.
- **E, quotes:** "Your quotes for request <n> (<k> of <N> businesses quoted so far). In the order they came in: [business, license number, price, when, note, Google link, Yelp link] ... For a handyman with no contractor license, in place of the license number: "Not a licensed contractor. Only for jobs under $1,000 total with no permit." (LIC-024, A.R.S. 32-1121(A)(14)). Reply with the one you pick, and say yes to sharing your first name, the job address and your email with that business. We never share your phone number. Ask Hava is not a contractor; you hire the business you pick directly."
- **F, introduction (free weeks):** "You were picked for job <n>. Homeowner: <first name>, <address>. Email: <her email>. Please get in touch within 24 hours. One question: if this weren't free, would this introduction have been worth $<fee> to you?"
- **G, not picked:** "Job <n> is no longer open. The homeowner picked another quote; it was <lower / about the same / higher> than yours. Thanks for quoting."
- **H, day 3:** "Did <business> get in touch about request <n>? Reply DONE, NO or PROBLEM."
- **I, after DONE:** "Thanks. May we share a line from your reply on askhava.com, with your first name and town? Yes or no is fine."
- **J, all quotes in:** "All quotes are in for request <n>: <k> of <N> businesses quoted. Reply with the one you pick, or CLOSE if you don't need it."
- **K, picked, paid weeks:** "You were picked for job <n>. The introduction fee is $<fee>: <payment link>. By paying, you agree to the Ask Hava Paid Pilot Terms dated <date>. Once it's paid, we introduce you: we email you the homeowner's first name, the job address and her email. It's a fixed fee for the introduction, not a share of the job, and it doesn't depend on her hiring you. If you email her twice, at least 3 hours apart, within 72 hours, and she never replies, tell us and we refund it."
- **L, receipt (paid weeks):** "Receipt: Ask Hava introduction service, job <n>, $<fee>, paid <date>. Under the Ask Hava Paid Pilot Terms dated <date>. Any tax owed is included."

The fee follows the real table (FEE-001): $25 when the quote is under $500, $75 from $500 up to $1,500, and $199 over $1,500. A range quote counts at its midpoint, a visit-fee quote pays $25, and ongoing services count 4 months of the monthly price, capped at $75 (FEE-039). In the paid weeks there's no free first win, because the point is to see real payment.

## 6. Getting homeowners (the demand plan)

Target: **at least 20 Organic requests over the 6 weeks, and 4 or more a week in each of weeks 4 to 6.** Seeded requests are welcome, but they test your process, not demand, so they don't count toward this.

1. **People you know first, for practice.** Ask friends, family and neighbors for real jobs they need quotes on. These are Seeded: use them in weeks 1 and 2 to smooth out your process.
2. **"Who do you recommend" threads.** About 3 replies a week, in neighborhood groups, only where someone is asking: "I run Ask Hava. If you want a few quotes to compare, you can send the job here: <link>. You pick, you hire them directly, it's free for you." Never name or praise a single business.
3. **Repeat askers.** Ask 3 to 5 property managers, vacation-rental hosts or realtors you know to send their repair jobs during the test. These are Partner requests: they test a second channel.
4. **One homeowner tip post a week** in neighborhood groups that allow it, with no link if the rules say so: "I run Ask Hava. Three things to ask before a water-heater swap: ..."
5. **Track the source** of every request with the tracking links (section 4, step 6) and the required "How did you hear" question.

## 7. Getting businesses (the supply plan)

Target: **in each pushed trade, 4 or more businesses signed up and 3 or more that have actually quoted by week 3,** and 10 or more self-serve sign-ups overall by week 6. Most businesses should find Ask Hava and sign up on their own; you visit only a few (section 8). Facebook groups are being tested here as a channel, not assumed to work.

- **Where:** local business-owner and trades groups first. A guest on Koerner's show found business groups far better than neighborhood groups for reaching businesses, where residents dislike ads (pack file 09, section 11). Neighborhood groups are for homeowners.
- **How often:** 3 to 5 posts a week, each reworded, in different groups, on different days. Never paste the same text twice. That stays well under Facebook's spam limits (pack file 10, section A1).
- **Ask first:** message each group's admin once before posting: what Ask Hava is, that it's free to join, and whether a business post is allowed or which thread to use.
- **Lead with pay-only-when-picked,** say "by email" plainly, and say from which date the fee applies.

**Post templates** (rotate and reword; plain words, no hype):

- **Intro (business groups):** "I run Ask Hava, a small local service here in Havasu. Homeowners send us a job; we email local businesses an anonymous job card, and you quote if you want. You pay a fixed fee only if the homeowner picks you, and it's free until <date>. Plumbers, pool techs, handymen and more: sign up here <link>."
- **Real requests (once you have them):** "I run Ask Hava. This week we had 4 pool-cleaning requests and only 1 pool business signed up. If you do weekly pool service in Havasu, sign up here: <link>." Counts must be true. Never claim how often a card turns into a job.
- **Question post:** "I run Ask Hava. Pool techs: would you quote a green-pool cleanup from three photos, or do you always need to see it first?" Save every answer as a future post.
- **Results (week 4 on):** "I run Ask Hava. Our first <weeks> weeks: <x> job cards sent, <y> businesses quoted, median first quote in <z> hours." True numbers only, with the date range.

**Pushed trades:** pick one or two at the start of week 1 and keep them for the whole test. In a winter start, one or two of handyman, heating and weekly pool cleaning; in a spring or summer start, one or two of AC repair, pool repair and pest control. All trades stay open; the push decides what your posts talk about and which trades the scorecard judges.

**Backup channel:** post the same way on Nextdoor if you have an account there, so one Facebook restriction doesn't stop the test.

## 8. The few visits, and the first-week welcome

- **When to visit (weeks 1 to 3):** a pushed trade has requests but fewer than 3 active businesses; or a business commented with interest but didn't sign up. Visit 2 or 3 shops with one question ("Would you quote a job like this from a description and photos?"), then point them to the sign-up form. In-person events: the Professionals Club of Havasu and the Chamber's building-industry expo (pack file 10, section C), as a guest. Log each visit as a nudge.
- **Welcome every new business yourself** with a short personal email within a day, and send them any open card in their trade at once. In weeks 1 to 3, check in after their first card: "Did the card have what you needed to quote?"
- **When a business stops quoting,** ask once why, and note the answer.
- **Weeks 4 to 6 are hands off:** standard emails only, no reminders. Comparing these weeks with weeks 1 to 3 shows whether Ask Hava works or you do.

## 9. Getting ready to take real fees (start in week 0)

The paid weeks need these in place by the end of week 3. If they aren't ready, run the paid weeks late (weeks 6 to 8). Don't mark payment as proven without them.

1. **Ask Hava LLC and its EIN** (`docs/PREBUILD.md` section 8). The filing is $50 at the Arizona Corporation Commission ($85 expedited). Use a statutory agent in Maricopa or Pima County: then the Commission publishes the notice itself, and no Mohave County newspaper notice is needed. The EIN is free. Check the fees on the Commission's site when you file.
2. **A live Stripe account** for the LLC, with payment links for $25, $75 and $199. Open it, and send the Stripe question, in week 0 or 1, so a refusal shows up before week 4, not at the first paid pick.
3. **Set aside 8.6% of every fee** for sales tax until the tax question is settled (D-444, D-470). The fee is probably not taxable, but no ruling settles it.
4. **The Lake Havasu City business license**, and the home-occupation form if you run it from home. Apply on the city's online business-license portal (linked from the city's Business License page); it's $196 new and $140 a year to renew (fee schedule effective Feb 1, 2026). It's separate from any sales-tax license. Get it before the first post if you can, and before week 4 at the latest. The home-occupation rules also bar putting your home address in any ad.
5. **The paid-pilot terms accepted** by every business that will get paid-week cards (section 4, step 7).
6. **A postal address** for the job-card footer: the LLC's agent or a mailbox, never your home address.

The sign-up form's note and template D tell businesses the fee date in advance, the paid-pilot terms say what the fee buys, and the payment link repeats the terms version. Refund in full under the terms' refund rule, and refund any other charge at your own choice (TERMS-026).

## 10. Scorecard and the decision

Fill in the Weekly tab every Sunday (about 15 minutes), with the week's dates, your minutes split into recruiting and running jobs, and the week's nudges. "Pushed" means the pushed trades only.

| Measure | Go to Gate P | Change the plan, or extend | Stop or rethink |
|---|---|---|---|
| Organic requests (all trades, 6 weeks) | 20 or more, and 4+ a week in weeks 4 to 6 | 10 to 19 | under 10 |
| Share of requests that aren't Seeded | 60% or more | 30% to 59% | under 30% |
| Pushed, routable requests with 1+ valid quote in 24 hours | 80% or more | 50% to 79% | under 50% |
| Pushed, routable requests with **2+** valid quotes in 24 hours | 60% or more | 30% to 59% | under 30% |
| Median time to first valid quote (pushed) | 6 hours or less | over 6, up to 12 hours | over 12 hours |
| 75th-percentile time to first valid quote (pushed) | 12 hours or less | over 12, up to 24 hours | over 24 hours |
| Picks, among requests with 2+ quotes | 35% or more | 20% to 34% | under 20% |
| Active businesses per pushed trade (have quoted) | 3 or more | 2 | 1 or fewer |
| Self-serve sign-ups, and the share that quoted at least once | 10 or more, 60%+ quoted | 30% to 59% quoted | under 30% quoted |
| **Real payments** (paid weeks) | 5 or more different businesses paid the real fee | fewer than 5 paid, too few to tell | repeated refusals at the real fee |
| Quote rate in hands-off weeks vs helped weeks | about the same | clearly lower | collapses without nudges |
| Your time (weeks 4 to 6) | about 30 minutes or less per request, and 5 hours a week or less | 30 to 45 minutes, or 5 to 8 hours | over 45 minutes, or over 8 hours |

**Go:** most lines in the first column, none in the last, **and** the real-payment line is green. Then reply `approved to build` at Gate P after the re-plan pass in section 12.
**Change the plan, or extend:** Claude makes the changes the numbers call for in one pass, or you run 2 to 4 more weeks to get enough paid introductions or Organic requests.
**Stop or rethink:** don't build yet. The numbers say the model needs to change.

Report the result as what it is: "In winter Havasu, in <pushed trades>, ..." A good winter result doesn't prove the yearly $20k to $50k. That needs about 150 requests a month at these rates.

The scorecard decides nothing on its own. You decide.

## 11. What the test answers for the build

Ask these in the first 5 to 10 business conversations and note the answers (the open interview items in `docs/for-casey-review.md`, section "Pending validation", C1 to C13):

- **C1:** Is the phone or email on your ROC record current? (The build's identity check sends codes there.)
- **C2:** "Sent to 6 businesses": at what number would you stop quoting?
- **C5 and C10:** How fast do you usually see an email? How many minutes does a quote like this take?
- **C6:** Is $25 fair for a small job, and would $35 still be? For ongoing service, is $75 fair for a new client? (Their answers are opinions; the paid weeks are the evidence.)
- **C13:** Does a 6-month price lock for early joiners matter to you?
- **C11:** Would hearing that the picked quote was lower make you quote again, or quit?
- **Worth quoting?** For each active business, note quotes sent, jobs won and its rough minutes per quote. A business that wins 1 in 10 and spends 45 minutes a quote will leave, however few others quote.

Also note, from homeowners: how many questions were too many, whether they sent photos, whether the card needed corrections, why anyone who didn't pick didn't, and whether anyone asked who runs Ask Hava.
