Subject: Eligibility check before A2P 10DLC registration: homeowner quote-request texts

Laftly LLC (laftly.com) is a quote-request service in Lake Havasu City, AZ. A homeowner texts our number to ask for quotes on a job at her own home. At launch, our public page shows the number and the SMS disclosures, and our first reply is the full consent text. Only Laftly texts her, and only about that request: intake questions, confirmations, 'your first quote is in' with a link to her private quote page, her pick confirmation, and the name and masked call-back number of the business she picked. We send no marketing. Our website chat, while texting is on, only shows our number and the SMS disclosures and asks her to text us; it collects no SMS consent. Local businesses get an anonymous job description by email (no name, phone, email or street address) and post quotes on our site. The homeowner may pick one quote. Only after she picks it, and that one business confirms and pays Laftly a fixed introduction fee ($25, $75 or $199 by job size; some jobs are fee-free), does Laftly show that business her first name and the job address so it can do the job she chose. Her phone number and her SMS consent are never given to any business; calls go through a masked number. No business sends texts on our number. We never sell or share lists.

1) Is this third-party lead generation under error 30951 or your forbidden-categories policy (10DLC or toll-free)? If it is eligible, which brand type and campaign use case should we register? We expect Low-Volume Standard / Low Volume Mixed.
2) Is a consumer's first inbound text, after a public page that shows our number and the SMS disclosures, acceptable opt-in for automated updates about her own request?
3) We reply in English, and in Spanish to people who write in Spanish. Should both languages and a Spanish sample be declared in the first registration?
4) If a reviewer applies 30951 in error, what is the appeal route, and can we reference this ticket in the submission?

Laftly LLC
