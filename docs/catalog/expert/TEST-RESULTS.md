**What was run.** Nine of 37 categories: the seven high-volume ones plus pool repair and roof repair. The other 28 have expert files and scenarios and no run. Each category ran the plan's scripted intake (`judges/intake_agent.md`: fixed question set in order, one expert follow-up, the set's three photos), the same model with no script (`baseline_no_playbook.md`) and a human-estimator persona (`baseline_human_estimator.md`). Four categories also ran `playbook_v2` (three rule tweaks to the script) and three ran `playbook_v3` (the expert file as guidance, the AI chooses its questions, cap of eight). The homeowner in every run is the same model following the scenario's hidden facts; the panel and the compliance judge are a stronger model scoring shuffled, unlabeled cards.

**What it says.** No category passes the bar under the scripted intake, and the failure is the same everywhere: the script asks what the job does not need, skips what it does, spends its one follow-up on the wrong thing, asks the wrong three photos, and re-asks what the opener already said (the commonest compliance hit, C11). The no-script baseline beats the script in every category, which means the script is costing quotable cards rather than adding them. Tweaking the script (v2) helped one category and hurt three. Letting the AI use the expert file (v3) brought plumbing to 100% quotable and 100% traps at five questions, electrical to 100% quotable and 87% traps at six questions, and hvac repair to 92% quotable and 93% traps at five questions; across the three it is 97% quotable against the script's 73% in the same categories.

**What it does not say.** Three cautions, in order of weight:

1. **One agent plays both the homeowner and the intake.** The run agent reads the scenario's hidden facts to play the homeowner, so it can leak a hidden fact onto the card without the "homeowner" ever typing it. The hvac v3 judge found six such lines; the pool repair judge found the same in the scripted run (s10). The C6 counts in the compliance blocks therefore measure leakage as well as invention, for every variant. A clean re-run needs two agents per scenario: one that holds the facts and answers, one that only sees the messages. `judges/README.md` records this as the first fix to the harness.
2. **v3's compliance is not better than the script's.** Plumbing v3 had 6 hits (script 5), electrical 19 (script 15), hvac 17 (script 13). The hits are re-asked facts, card lines she did not say (see 1), and two or three advice-like lines that came from trade-brief photo wording ("do not remove any covers", "if you can reach it safely", "please do not climb the roof"). The guided intake needs a never-re-ask check and a scrub of the trade briefs' photo asks before it is as clean as the script; the rule proposal says so.
3. **Model-played homeowners.** Scripted homeowners answer every question and never wander, misread or stop replying. Real homeowners do all three, which favors fewer questions. The hand-run test with real people (D-455, D-468) is the only measure of that.

**The pass bar was not met by any variant** when compliance is counted: the bar needs zero violations, and even the best run has at least five.
