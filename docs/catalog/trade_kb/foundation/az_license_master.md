# Arizona License Master: Ask Hava Trade KB (Lake Havasu City, AZ)

Purpose: one shared reference so every trade file uses the same license class numbers, the same handyman rule, and the same agency names. Trade YAML files copy from here; they must not invent class numbers.

How to read this file
- [S#] = numbered source in `sources_foundation.md`.
- **Verified** = checked against the rule or statute text retrieved for this KB.
- **[judgment]** = our reading, not stated in a source. Treat it as a starting point, not a fact.
- **[unverified]** = we could not confirm it from a primary source. Check it before using it in any message.
- Ask Hava does not check, rank, or vouch for licenses. This file helps us write neutral questions and job cards, and helps businesses see which jobs fit them. A homeowner can look up any license on the AZ ROC site. Our neutral wording: "You can look up any contractor license at roc.az.gov."

Text version used
- A.A.C. R4-9-102, R4-9-103, R4-9-104 (Registrar of Contractors). Official publisher: Arizona Secretary of State, Title 4 Ch. 9 [S1]. The SOS PDF and roc.az.gov blocked automated fetches, so the text was read from the Cornell LII copy [S1][S2][S3]. Last amendment shown: effective 11/5/2017. Justia lists the rules as current through Register Vol. 31, No. 25 (June 20, 2025) [S40]. We found no newer amendment.
- A.R.S. 32-1121, read from azleg.gov [S4].

Many "R"-suffix subclasses (e.g. R-37R Gas Piping, R-39R Evaporative Cooling and Ventilators) no longer accept new applications, but existing holders may still carry them [S2]. A business may show an older class. That is normal.

## 4. Handyman exemption: A.R.S. 32-1121(A)(14) [S4]

Exact text (verified):
> "Any person other than a licensed contractor engaging in any work or operation on one undertaking or project by one or more contracts, for which the aggregate contract price, including labor, materials and all other items, but excluding any electrical fixture or appliance that was designed by the manufacturer, that is unaltered, unchanged or unmodified by any person, that can be plugged into a common household electrical outlet ... is less than $1,000. The work or operations that are exempt under this paragraph shall be of a casual or minor nature. This exemption does not apply:
> (a) In any case in which the performance of the work requires a local building permit.
> (b) In any case in which the work or construction is only a part of a larger or major operation, whether undertaken by the same or a different contractor, or in which a division of the operation is made in contracts of amounts less than $1,000 ... for the purpose of evasion of this chapter or otherwise.
> (c) To a person who uses any form of advertising to the public in which the person's unlicensed status is not disclosed by including the words 'not a licensed contractor' in the advertisement."

Subsection (D) (verified) says the (A)(4), (A)(9) and (A)(14) exemptions do **not** apply to:
1. Fire-safety work, for example **hardwired or interconnected smoke alarms** and **fire sprinklers**.
2. **Any work that connects to natural gas, propane, or another gaseous fuel.**

Subsection (C) (verified): a person who does not make the (14)(c) disclosure can face action under the consumer fraud law (A.R.S. 44-1522).

### Plain rules for the KB (all four must be true for "handyman_ok: true")
1. **Under $1,000 total**, counting labor and materials. A plug-in appliance or fixture sold as-is does not count toward the total. Example: a store-bought plug-in microwave.
2. **No city permit needed.** Use the Lake Havasu City list in section 7.
3. **Not part of a larger job**, and not split into small invoices to stay under $1,000.
4. **No gas** (natural gas or propane) **and no fire-safety systems** (hardwired or interconnected smoke alarms, sprinklers).
Also: ads by an unlicensed handyman must say "not a licensed contractor."

How Ask Hava uses this [judgment]
- The job card shows the facts (job type, rough size, whether a permit list item applies). It does not say who may legally do the job.
- In the YAML, `handyman_ok` is a routing hint for which businesses see a card. It is not legal advice.
- Never tell a homeowner "a handyman can do this." Neutral wording: "Some small jobs under $1,000 that don't need a permit can be done by a handyman. Bigger or permitted jobs need a licensed contractor. You can look up any license at roc.az.gov."

## 6. Non-ROC agencies

### Pest control: AZ Department of Agriculture, Pest Management Division (PMD) [S9][S10]
- **Business license:** A.R.S. 3-3615 requires a business license from the division ✔.
- **Applicator categories** (A.R.S. 3-3614(B)) ✔:
  1. Industrial, institutional, structural and health-related
  2. Wood-destroying organism management (termites)
  3. Ornamental and turf (includes pre-emergent weed control in yards [judgment])
  4. Right-of-way
  5. Aquatic
  6. Fumigation
  7. Others set by rule
- Older category codes still seen in ads: B1 General Pest, B2 Wood-Destroying Organisms, B3 Weeds/Right-of-way, B4 Fumigation, B5 Turf & Ornamental, B9 Aquatic [S47; third-party, unverified].
- **Owner exemption** (A.R.S. 3-3611(A)(1)): a homeowner using nonrestricted pesticides on their own home needs no license ✔.
- **Pool exemption** (3-3611(A)(5)): over-the-counter antimicrobial pool treatment needs no license ✔.
- Termite treatments are reported to PMD (the eTARF termite action reporting system) [S10; snippet only].

## 7. What needs a permit in Lake Havasu City [S17] (verified list)

**Permit REQUIRED**
- Detached structures over 200 sq ft, or more than one under 200 sq ft
- Electrical, mechanical and plumbing work for detached structures under 200 sq ft
- Room additions; patio covers; porch and carport enclosures
- Retaining walls over 2 ft 6 in (with slope) or over 3 ft 0 in (no slope)
- Pools, spas, above-ground pools
- Gas lines
- Window replacement **when the size changes**
- Replacing roof **sheathing**
- Removing or installing interior or exterior walls
- Drywall (new or replacement, not hole patching)
- Adding or relocating plumbing fixtures; water re-pipes
- Electrical panel change-outs; electrical updates and rewires
- Photovoltaic (solar) systems
- Fireplaces
- Lath for stucco

**Permit NOT required**
- Paint; floor covering
- Minor drywall repair (hole patching)
- Minor non-structural repairs (glass, trim, cabinets, hardware)
- Replacing roof **covering**
- Replacing existing plumbing fixtures
- Repairing or replacing an existing AC **without** increasing weight, capacity, location or ampacity
- Replacing an existing water heater
- Water softeners and RO systems
- Privacy walls up to 6 ft

Use in YAML [judgment]: if a job type is on the REQUIRED list, set `handyman_ok: false` because exemption (14)(a) fails.

## 8. Who owns which pipe (LHC) [S19][S20] (verified)

> "Your responsibility for your property's water service lateral begins at the point where it connects to the City-owned water meter ... Your responsibility for your sewer service lateral begins at the point where it connects to the City's sewer main in the street."

So a sewer backup anywhere between the house and the main **in the street** is usually the owner's line. Only the main itself is the city's.
- Neutral routing wording: "If it looks like the issue is in the city's water meter or the sewer main in the street, you can call Lake Havasu City Wastewater at [number removed, see source]. For your side of the line, we can send a request to local plumbers."
- Note: the city pages say "City of Kingman" in this paragraph, which looks like a copy error on the city site.

LHC code requires pressure regulators. The city says pressure entering a home "should be below 60 pounds per square inch (PSI)" [S19]. This is useful for "my pipes bang / leaks keep happening" questions. Ask, don't diagnose.
