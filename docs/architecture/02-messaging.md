# Architecture section 2 notes: `messaging`

Moved here verbatim from `docs/architecture.md` (D-6022), whose index lists every part. Until T885 makes `docs/architecture/` a shared path, a task adds new notes to the index, at the end of the section this part came from, and the next plan change moves them here.

The section 2 notes ("Processes and code layout") whose first named crate is `messaging`, in their original order.

- Text outbox: every text is queued through `messaging::api::enqueue` under an idempotency key derived (`sms.send`, `ID_SECRET`) from its template ID, recipient ID and subject IDs, unique in `messaging.outbox`; `send` claims only a `queued` row in one statement (`sending`, `sending_since`), calls the `SmsAdapter` outside any transaction with the key re-derived from the stored IDs, then marks it sent and clears its body (D-850); Twilio does not dedupe by key (D-558), so a row left `sending` by a crash or an unknown answer is never resent: the outbox sender (`messaging.send_due`, T356, D-2628) marks it `unknown` after 5 minutes, and re-checks quiet hours and the line before every send (TXT-011); a homeowner's number is read from the vault for the row's recipient at send time, a business phone is given by the caller, and a send must name the recipient kind the row was queued for (D-849); a past customer is texted only after she wrote since her request closed, from times the caller supplies, checked at enqueue and again at send (TXT-009). `messaging.sender_directory` keeps each business main or staff phone as `sender-dir` blind-index digests for `sender_add`, `sender_remove` and `sender_business` (T028, D-492, D-846, D-847, D-849, D-850).
- Text retries and email (T094, D-1378 to D-1381): a failed text is retried 10, 20 and 30 minutes after its first failure (`messaging::api::record_failure`); then `give_up` marks it `failed` and, in one transaction, lists an important text in `messaging.portal_notice` and queues it as an email when an address is on file, or records a call-back (`messaging.text_retry`, `ops.alert` `messaging.call_back_due`, due 24 hours urgent, 4 days otherwise) for a homeowner with none (TXT-014, TXT-015, INT-023). Every email goes once through `messaging.email_outbox` (`queue_email`, `send_email`, key `email.send` over template, recipient and subject IDs, no address stored), rendered in `app/messaging/templates/email_layout.html` with `lang` and the SEC-028 footer; `Bulk` emails wait for the midnight-UTC reset above the daily limit's share, a provider 429 waits and is never dropped (OPS-038). Verified Resend webhooks are stored once in `messaging.email_event` in event-time order, a complaint and 24 hours of silence raise alerts (SEC-050, SEC-051). The homeowner's update channel is `messaging.update_channel` (TXT-018). Every queued email no handler sends itself goes through the drain `messaging.email_due` (T470, D-3750, D-4327, D-4670 to D-4673): each minute it leases at most 6 due rows in one `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` statement (`sending` with `claimed_at`; a lease is retaken only after 5 minutes), never an owner alert email and a self-sending template only once held, drops a row holding `LINK_MARK` or `CODE_MARK`, one 23 hours past its first unsure call (`first_unsure_at`) or one with none queued 72 hours (status `dropped`, bodies cleared, one High alert), sends through `send_email` with the one provider and daily limit, backs off 1 minute doubling to 60 (`lease_count`) after an unsure or setup answer, and a 400 or 422 drops the row; business addresses come from the server's `email_due::NoticeAddresses` (a named business that has not left, a current member's or verified backup's mailbox, `BusinessPurpose::Notice`; the owner inbox for `OWNER_INBOX` rows). Not wired yet: the Resend webhook route and the `core.task` email request for modules above `messaging` (D-1380).
- Delivery and coverage alerts (T105, D-3910): `messaging`'s status callback, `businesses`' status changes and edits, and `jobs`' hourly `jobs.request_counts` task report counts to `ops::api` (`record_delivery_counts`, `record_business_counts` under `lock_coverage`, `record_request_counts`); `ops` keeps them in `ops.delivery_alert`, `ops.delivery_line`, `ops.coverage` and `ops.trade_request`, acknowledges a recovered delivery alert in `ops.alert_ack`, and holds `ops.alert_push` and `ops.safe_mode` for T487 (migration 0192).
- Inbox and delivery statuses: `messaging::api::receive_inbound` and `receive_status` take only a `VerifiedWebhook` from Twilio and the instant from the caller's injected clock (stored as every row's `received_at`, no database default, D-863), parse MessageSid (`SM`/`MM` plus 32), MessageStatus and ErrorCode once, and record the event in `messaging.webhook_event` (source `twilio_inbound` keyed on the MessageSid, `twilio_status` on (MessageSid, MessageStatus), SEC-039) in the same transaction as its effect, so a repeat changes nothing (TXT-012): the first inbound delivery makes one `messaging.inbox` row, the first status callback appends a `messaging.delivery_event` (status folded to queued, sending, sent, delivered, undelivered, failed, with the error code). `messaging::api::delivery_status` reads a text's status from its outbox row and the events for its provider SID: never moved back by a late earlier status, so a callback that arrived before the send was marked still counts (TXT-013, T178, D-861).
- Opt-outs and the one suppression list: `messaging::api::on_inbound_text` takes an inbound text's scrubbed words, its `OptOutType` and emergency flag; `rules::detect_opt_out` (sole word or listed phrase, CON-008, CON-010, CON-011) or the provider's STOP lists the number in `messaging.suppression` (digests under the `homeowner-phone` index key) and logs `messaging.optout_event` (channel, how detected, time; append-only) in one transaction; the app queues the CON-009 confirmation only for words the provider did not answer, on the first listing (the one whose insert added the suppression rows) and while texting is not paused; only the provider's `OptOutType=START` (START or UNSTOP) lifts it; anything else from a listed number sends nothing, and an emergency flag on any message from it raises a critical `ops` alert. `messaging::api::suppress` is the entry for every other revocation channel. `messaging::api::send_checked` is the send every text takes: it refuses a listed number except the HELP reply and the current listing's confirmation within 5 minutes, and a 21610 refusal lists the number and marks the outbox row `blocked`, never claimed again (T029, CON-013 to CON-017, CON-030, CON-035, D-866 to D-868, D-870).
- Message log and body cut (T093, D-1851 to D-1854; TXT-019, TXT-020, CON-036): `messaging.outbox` is the outbound message log, holding the recipient's vault reference (never the number), template ID, recipient kind, the body's `language` (set at enqueue by matching the body against the template, `retention::language_of`; NULL when it fits neither or both), the request among `subject_ids`, `sent_at`, `attempts` and Twilio's status via `provider_sid`; `messaging::api::message_log` reads one entry as `LogEntry`. A sent row keeps no body (SEC-037); the daily `messaging.cut_bodies` task (`run_cut`, scheduled by the worker at start and by each run) cuts the body of any row still unsent 30 days after it was queued and ends it `failed`. No log row is deleted. `messaging.inbox` stores no words. A texted opt-out's `messaging.optout_event` row keeps its detection and the `inbox_id` of the message that carried it (migration 0128). The day-31 delete of Twilio's own copy is `messaging`'s `purge.rs` (T276, D-2160): the daily `messaging.body_delete_scan` queues one `messaging.body_delete` task per inbound or outbound SID 31 or more days old, deduped by SID, which redacts the body through `SmsBodyDelete` (a 20404 counts as done, any other error retries). The 5-year end of the log (CON-022) is `messaging::consent_purge` (T321, D-4168): `messaging.inbox_contact` records each inbound text's contact, `messaging.last_message_at` reads a number's last message, `messaging.purge_expired_optout_events` (`SECURITY DEFINER`) deletes its old unreferenced opt-out events but the newest, and `delete_message_log` its outbox and inbox rows, all strictly past 1,827 days; T563's daily sweep calls them.
- HELP, INFO and AYUDA (T030, D-887 to D-890): `messaging::api::reply_to_help` queues the CON-018 reply for a text that is only one of those words, under template ID `help_reply`, which `rules::may_send_to` never lets through to an opted-out number (the sender expires it; D-5280); `HelpSettings` carries the reply times and the owner-away date; no voice number is ever given.
- Text templates: `app/messaging/templates/en.json` and `es.json` are the registry of every fixed text (TXT-001, TXT-004), one key per template ID with both languages and a recorded `segments` count; each task adds only its own keys (D-759, D-491).
- Weekly opt-out rates (T107, D-3880, D-3881, D-4082, D-4059; CON-022 to CON-024): `messaging::consent_alerts` counts each Arizona week (Monday 00:00 to Monday 00:00, UTC-7, `messaging::api::Week`) by audience: texted is the distinct `recipient_id` of sent `messaging.outbox` rows, opted out the distinct digests whose `messaging.optout_event` newly listed the number (a repeat STOP is not counted; STOP, START, STOP is), a business's being one whose inbox row has the `businesses.phone_stopped` audit row (read through `ops::api::audited`). The weekly core task `messaging.optout_rate_check` (registered in `server::worker_registry`, first queued by `schedule_start` under a per-week dedupe key) checks the week just ended and raises one Normal `messaging.optout_rate_homeowners` (3%) or `messaging.optout_rate_businesses` (5%) alert per week through `ops::api::raise_alert_once`, subject `messaging.optout_week`. T108's dashboard and T524's digest read `weekly_optout_rates`; counts only leave `messaging`. No new table.
- Spanish conversations switch stored (T480, D-4756, D-4757; TXT-039, TXT-043, SEC-043, D-427, D-3844): new append-only table `messaging.spanish_switch` (migration 0208; app role SELECT and INSERT only; on rows hold the professional review's date, reviewer and `reviewed` JSON of Spanish template ID to version, the campaign note's date and reference and the Spanish sample; off rows none; the newest row is in force, none is off). A version is the lowercase hex SHA256 of the Spanish text from `Registry::template(id, Language::Es)`. `messaging::spanish_switch` gives `spanish_switch(conn)` (an on row reads on only while its review covers every current version), `spanish_template_versions`, `turn_spanish_on(conn, owner, &SpanishRecords, now)` (refused, storing nothing, with each `Missing` item) and `turn_spanish_off`, each change one row and one `messaging.spanish_switch_changed` audit row (IDs and on/off only) under an advisory lock. Readers: `server::inbound_text::switched` per message (`spanish_conversations_on`; the retry reaches it through `rerun`) and `portals::owner_portal`, whose `POST /owner/spanish-conversations` (`owner_spanish`, fresh factor `OwnerAction::Settings`) turns it on or off; a refusal is a 400 naming each missing record. Section 3's `messaging` table list does not yet name the table (locked; D-4757).
- Business-texting eligibility gate (T527, D-4812, D-4813; TXT-035, TERMS-014, SEC-043, D-303, D-305, D-3881): new append-only table `messaging.business_eligibility` (migration 0209; app role SELECT and INSERT only; each row the provider answer's date, reference and text, `allowed`, the business campaign reference or null, `filed_by`, `filed_at`; the newest by `seq` is in force). New `messaging::business_eligibility`: `file_business_eligibility` (one row and one `messaging.business_eligibility_filed` audit row, IDs only; an answer that does not permit business texting also switches the business line off, under `messaging.mode_state`'s lock) and `business_eligibility` (the newest filing). `messaging::api::switch_on` for `Audience::Business` now answers the new `ModeStateError::NotEligible` unless that filing says allowed and names a campaign; the homeowner line is unchanged. `portals::owner_business_texting` (fresh factor `OwnerAction::Settings`) serves `POST /owner/business-texting` (`to` = `file`, `on` or `off`) through `server::owner_portal`; the settings page shows the switch, the newest answer and why the switch cannot go on; a refused turn-on is a 400. Section 3's `messaging` table list does not yet name the table (locked; D-4757's reasoning).
- Failed-text call-back urgency (T581, D-4052, D-5179; INT-023, TXT-015, OPS-032): `messaging` may not read `intake`, so `messaging::api::UrgencySource` (a boxed-future trait in `outbox_sender.rs`) answers a homeowner contact's `Urgency`; `OutboxSender::with_urgency` sets it and none means no rush. `server::outbox_sender` passes `IntakeUrgency` over `intake::api::contact_urgent(db, contact, now)` (her open request, else her latest closed within `CALL_BACK_DAYS`, judged by `handlers::request_urgent`); a read error holds the row.
