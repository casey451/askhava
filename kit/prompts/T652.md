OPS-001 (`docs/spec/ops-recovery.md:5`), D-075, D-942 and D-1719 name the set: an OPS-004 alert other than the email sending-limit one, or a critical alert. Add one `pub const SAFE_MODE_KINDS: [&str; 4]` = `ops.vendor_errors`, `ops.health_check_failed`, `payments.ledger_mismatch`, `messaging.number_suspended`, and make all three clauses `(a.severity = 'critical' OR a.kind = ANY($n))`, still for an open (`resolved_at IS NULL`) alert with an `ops.alert_push` row; the delivery alert (`ops.delivery_low`, OPS-001's own exception), `ops.email_limit`, both vault kinds and `messaging.call_back_due` never start it unless raised critical, and none of them is. Each `SAFE_MODE_KINDS` entry is the constant its raiser in `ops` uses (a unit test asserts equality where `ops` can name it, and the string otherwise, as D-4087 did). Keep `safe_mode_on`'s fail-closed read by the database's `now()` (D-3878) and the on and off audit rows (D-4000 (2), (3)); D-4000 (1)'s severity test and D-4087's "never started safe mode" clause are superseded by D-4310 (D-4087's push listing stays). Correct the docs that say "a pushed High alert" (`:41` to `:43`, `:58` to `:61`, the `SAFE_MODE_AFTER` doc) and the `app/ops/NOTES.md` line, if any. Accepted risk (Casey's review list): a vault spike Casey leaves for 24 hours no longer pauses charges; safe mode never contained vault reads. (2) Delivery hold per line (OPS-025, D-163; OPS-004 counts per audience): `raise_delivery`'s `held` (`alerts.rs:188` to `:195`) compares the newest resolved delivery alert of any audience with this line's `recovered_at`, so after the homeowner line's alert is acknowledged, a business line that dropped while it was open is held until it alone reaches 97%, and its outage is never alerted.
